# Canada 1901 racial or tribal origin

The 1905 official Canada Year Book, Table I, printed pages 4–12, reports the March 31, 1901 census results. Use the 1901 column headed “By race or origin” for the seven provinces, The Territories and Unorganized Territories. The observations are from the de jure census. The eight accounts close to 5,371,315 residents at the 1901 denominator. [Official yearbook](https://www66.statcan.gc.ca/eng/1905-eng.htm) · [Combined scan](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2017/statcan/11-202/CS11-202-1905-eng.pdf)

Schedule 1, column 14 recorded “Racial or tribal origin.” Nationality was column 15. The manual traced European origin through the father, required tribal names for Indigenous returns, and provided codes for mixed European and Indigenous descent. The archival guide describes Indigenous descent as traced through the mother. The tabulation measures ancestry under these historical reporting rules. [1901 census instructions, paragraphs 53–54](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2018/parl/x12-3/X12-3-A-1903-eng.pdf) · [Library and Archives Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/genealogy-family-history/censuses/dominion-canada/1901.html)

- `origin_1901_yearbook_table_i.csv` retains the printed province and territory rows. Historical wording is retained and the punctuation of “Indian and h. b.” is standardized. The printed heading combines the census Indigenous and mixed European–Indigenous rows. New Brunswick p. 6 gives Dutch 3,663 and this combined category 1,465. Ontario p. 8 gives 24,665 for the combined category, consistent with the original census volume's errata (19,670 + 4,995). Prince Edward Island p. 9 gives Italian 12.
- `origin_1901_opening_scopes.csv` combines Quebec (1,648,898) and Unorganized Territories (52,709) using the account's 1901 endpoint geography. The Territories (158,940) uses the contemporary organized territory boundaries. Newfoundland and Labrador was outside the census of the Dominion. The 1912 retrospective presentation uses later western provincial boundaries.
- Five origin rows are common to every printed region and are carried as published. “Other specified origins” combines the remaining published origins and the yearbook’s “Other races.” Its internal composition varies by region. “Not given” remains unknown. There are 31,539 recorded without a stated origin, including 16,763 in Unorganized Territories.
- Initial enumerators used a limited form in Unorganized Territories. The census introductions and archive guide describe incomplete coverage of some Indigenous communities. People omitted altogether are outside the observed denominator. The files preserve printed totals and contain no estimate of the omitted population. The 1900 opening allocation, if used, should be identified as a dated share-based model.
