# Philippine Islands census of 1903

The census date is 2 March 1903 (Volume II, p. 15). General Table 1 (p. 123)
reports a national control and 50 non-overlapping census reporting areas:
39 provinces, 9 military districts (comandancias), Manila city, and the
separately returned Marinduque subprovince. The Tayabas row excludes
Marinduque. The table follows 1903 reporting boundaries.

| Scope | Total | “Civilized” | “Wild” |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Published archipelago control | 7,635,426 | 6,987,686 | 647,740 |
| Sum of the 50 reporting areas | 7,635,426 | 6,987,686 | 647,740 |

The quotation marks identify the census's colonial categories. The second
category includes grouped village enumeration and estimates (Vol. II, p. 9).
Zero in the transcription represents a blank or leader in that column when
the printed total equals the other category. The two columns are components
of each row's total.

The transcription was checked against the retained scan of p. 123, the
supporting 1905 summary on p. 16, and the 1904 Bulletin 1, p. 14. The
University of Michigan scan labels the source page 123, image 149 in the
digitized volume. In the automatic extract of p. 123, Cebú
(658,727 / 653,727 / 5,000), Iloilo (403,992 in the “Civilized” column),
Sorsogón (120,464 / 31) and Tawi Tawi (14,688 total) disagree with those
checks. The selected readings close to the national controls, and
Sorsogón's 120,454 also appears in Vol. II, Table 3. Use
`python build.py` to rebuild these observations offline.

The 1898 treaty boundary and the reporting area in 1903 differ. The treaty
signed on 7 November 1900 included Cagayan Sulu and Sibutu and their
dependencies. It entered into force on 23 March 1901. The 1903 island table
lists 2,000 for Cagayán de Jolo (Mapun) and 280 for Sibutu. The 1903 archipelago control totals 7,635,426 in both the census and the historical study. It is a shared benchmark. The 1900 count
for those islands and their dependencies is unknown.
