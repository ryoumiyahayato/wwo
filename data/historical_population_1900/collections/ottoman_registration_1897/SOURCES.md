# Ottoman population registration, Rumi 1313

## Sources

- Ottoman General Statistical Administration, *Devlet-i Aliyye-i Osmaniyye’nin 1313 Senesine Mahsus İstatistik-i Umumisi*, Istanbul, published 1900. Tevfik Güran’s 1997 State Institute of Statistics edition lists population in tables 5–13, printed pp. 19–29. The [official reissue front matter and table list](https://www.library.illinois.edu/ias/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2016/12/Historical_Statistics_Series_Vol_5.pdf#page=21) identifies the tables. The open PDF ends before the tables. The [Turkish Historical Society catalogue](https://kutuphane.ttk.gov.tr/details?id=429690&materialType=KT&query=Tevfik.) identifies the original publication.
- Tevfik Güran, *1897 Tarihli İlk İstatistik Yıllığı*, State Institute of Statistics, 2000. [Institutional record and PDF](https://makale.isam.org.tr/items/3ec91b3b-5c56-404f-abe8-7a6fdae6f470). The yearbook editor reproduces the figures in Table 2, printed p. 173, and Tables 3–4, printed p. 174. [Table 2](https://makale.isam.org.tr/server/api/core/bitstreams/710bc80b-1f62-405a-a921-39e789b56fa7/content#page=11) and [Tables 3–4](https://makale.isam.org.tr/server/api/core/bitstreams/710bc80b-1f62-405a-a921-39e789b56fa7/content#page=12).
- Shaw, *The Ottoman Census System and Population, 1831–1914*, pp. 329–332, gives the distinction between a standing civil population register and military returns. Under the 1874 predecessor, temporary soldiers and police were registered in their home districts. The 1881 regime retained the system of permanent local registers, added military status to male records, and allowed absent men to register by proxy. The exact treatment of garrisons in the 1897 tables remains unverified. [Article](https://md.teyit.org/file/shaw-the-ottoman-census-system.pdf#page=5).

## Coverage and use

The fiscal reporting period was 1897-03-13 to 1898-03-12. The 1897 entries summarize a rolling register using reports of different dates. The fiscal year-end is a reporting anchor 660 days before opening. Some counts are older. Mutlu documents the same figures for selected Istanbul communities in 1895 and 1897, revisions in Edirne and underregistration of women and children. [Mutlu, pp. 12–13](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/213586#page=12).

- The 33 administrative rows in `registered_population_by_administration_1897.csv` total 19,050,321. Men 10,104,038 and women 8,946,283 independently close. Muslim 14,111,960 and non-Muslim 4,938,361 independently close. The 30 opening partitions report 13,674,483 Muslims and 4,910,184 non-Muslims, as a separate registration marginal. Only Aydın (745,614 men and 732,810 women) and Ankara (516,964 men and 501,780 women) supply sex-only opening controls. Mutlu singles out their adult register sex ratios as broadly plausible. He documents underregistration of children in Aydın. The opening model applies all-age reported sex counts only to these recorded persons. [Mutlu, pp. 12–13](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/213586#page=12).
- The 1897 annual register line in Table 3 has a separate total of 19,050,307. Men 10,104,022 and women 8,946,285 close. Muslim 14,111,945 and non-Muslim 4,938,362 close. The administrative table exceeds this line by 14. Preserve the two controls as published.
- The 19 published age bands in `registered_age_sex_1897.csv` total 19,050,308, one above the annual register. The published first sex column sums to 10,104,013 and the second to 8,946,295. The headings call the first women and the second men. The column sums indicate a likely transposition. The yearbook editor's [second reproduction, Table 9.4](https://www.scribd.com/document/681126521/Tevfik-Guran-19-Yuzy%C4%B1lda-Osmanl%C4%B1-Ekonomisi-Uzerine-Ara%C5%9Ft%C4%B1rmalar) repeats the labels. The last printed label is 90+, after 86–90. The CSV preserves the original column positions and bands as archival data. Opening ages remain unknown.
- `registered_communities_1897.csv` records twelve mutually exclusive source labels. The Ottoman aggregate community table at original p. 16 appears in [Zeki Taşkesen’s archival transcription, pp. 1063–64](https://isamveri.org/pdfdrg/D138879/2004/2004_TASKZ.pdf). Its Muslim male count is printed as 8,499,798. Use the independently reproduced 7,499,798 from [the Shaw-derived Table 2, pp. 48–49](https://otk.org.pl/wp-content/uploads/Biuletyn-112_113.pdf), which closes with both the published Muslim total and the annual male count. The source labels mix confession and community. Preserve the distinction between Katolik and Latin. Preserve Kıpti gayr-i müslim as a mixed historical label.
- Foreign residents and Ottoman citizens abroad appear as separate yearbook tables 7–8, pp. 20–21. Their exact overlap with the registered denominators remains unverified. Avoid adding either count. The 1897 registration excludes or has major gaps in Crete, Tripolitania, Benghazi, Mount Lebanon, Hejaz and Yemen. Egypt, Tunisia, Bosnia-Herzegovina, Bulgaria and Cyprus have distinct administrative accounts or scopes. Crete is separate from the Archipelago vilayet in the yearbook. The Archipelago line identifies Lesbos, Chios, Rhodes and Lemnos and excludes Crete. [Official reissue, printed p. XVIII](https://www.library.illinois.edu/ias/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2016/12/Historical_Statistics_Series_Vol_5.pdf#page=16), [Mutlu, Table III](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/213586#page=15).
- Baghdad (187,285), Basra (80,081) and Mosul (198,288) total 465,654 registered people. Their reported female counts were 7,292, 14 and 6,988. Treat all three as partial records. Their observed sex distributions are unsuitable as estimates for resident populations. Table 2’s 30 remaining rows total 18,584,667. Several also have known undercounts. [Karpat, table I.18 and notes](https://www.osmanlipostatarihi.org/panel/uploads/makale/pdf/52.pdf#page=2), [McCarthy, Iraq population registers](https://dokumen.pub/population-history-of-the-middle-east-and-the-balkans-9781463225605.html).
- The 24,000,000 legacy opening control debits 18,584,667 to the 30 eligible register accounts. The remaining 5,415,333 retains the legacy basis and has no geographic allocation. The balance measures an accounting difference. Counts remain on their 1897 boundaries as statistical proxies. Later growth, boundary changes and omitted residents are unquantified. The Asian FT figure (25,646,777), Balkan figure (4,407,683) and Tripolitania/Cyrenaica figure (766,146) remain separate research scopes.

Şehr Emaneti Mülhakatı is a separate outlying municipal area. Çatalca is a separate line. Only `place:istanbul` was verified in the examined game map identities. [Karpat, table I.18](https://www.osmanlipostatarihi.org/panel/uploads/makale/pdf/52.pdf#page=2).
