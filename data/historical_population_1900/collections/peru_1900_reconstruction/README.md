# Peru population near 1900

The INEI historical series places Peru at 3,383,000 in 1900. The v26 national account uses this annual estimate as its opening proxy. The table gives a year in thousands of people. The reference day, estimate procedure, treatment of missed enumeration, and correspondence to the 1900 Chilean administrative frontier are unspecified. `observations.jsonl` retains year precision. [INEI, sec. 1.7](https://proyectos.inei.gob.pe/web/biblioineipub/bancopub/Est/Lib0363/cap1.htm)

## Department and coastal province table

`sinopsis_1899_department_population.csv` transcribes the 20 administrative totals and the national control. The 95 provinces and 798 districts sum to 2,660,881 for the 1876 census column and 4,609,999 for the column calculated in 1896. Peru's 1876 census first published its own provincial boundaries and Tarapacá. The 1899 compendium table reflects later boundaries and leaves Tarapacá outside both columns. The 20 local entries, including the administratively distinct coastal provinces Callao and Moquegua, close exactly to the printed national line. [Sinopsis, printed pp. 24–27](https://books.google.com/books?id=CiswAAAAYAAJ&pg=PA24)

The printed annual percentage is consistent with the annual compound change between the reported 1876 and 1896 columns. For example, Callao moves from 34,492 to 48,118 and prints 1.68% annual and 39.5% total. This relationship cannot reproduce the estimated 1896 counts from independent inputs. A contemporary account says the Sociedad Geográfica de Lima's commission consulted official records compiled in 17 volumes, used the 1876 census and subsequent demographic statistics, and reported in 1897. Its printed total of 4,609,881 differs by 118 from the 1899 compendium. The counts and published totals retain the figure of their own edition. [Sociedad Geográfica de Lima, vols. XIII–XIV, printed p. 94](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/29/Bolet%C3%ADn_de_la_Sociedad_Geogr%C3%A1fica_de_Lima._%28IA_boletndelasocied1314soci%29.pdf)

`research_19_subnational_rows.csv` retains the 19 entries outside the combined Tacna department. Their published 1876 counts sum to 2,624,872 and their 1896 estimates sum to 4,559,550. The 1896 sum still exceeds the 1900 INEI national estimate by 1,176,550. The rows can support a research comparison of geographic shares. There is no established province-by-province underenumeration adjustment. The Loreto row represents five published provinces, including San Martín and Moyobamba. The June 1897 progress report says 11 prefects had supplied all requested local data and the other units had supplied partial data, except Loreto, which had supplied none from any of its five provinces. The published Loreto estimate therefore lacks a confirmed complete contemporary local return. The number of people beyond effective enumeration is unknown. [Luis Carranza, Memoria anual, vol. VII, printed p. 50](https://archive.org/stream/boletndelasocied7189soci/boletndelasocied7189soci_djvu.txt) The Piura row includes Tumbes. Keep 1900 geographic uncertainty explicit. ## Southern frontier

The 1883 Treaty of Ancón permanently ceded Tarapacá to Chile. Chile retained administration over most of the Peruvian Tacna and Arica territory described in Article 3. As of 1900 the plebiscite remained outstanding. The Sinopsis keeps Tacna as a combined department with Arica, Tacna and Tarata and notes disruption of the Tacna court under the occupation. The three source rows close to 36,009 in 1876 and 50,449 in 1896. In the 1925 arbitration, the documents distinguish Chile's effective jurisdiction over Tarata, Tarucachi and Estique from the remaining Peruvian Tarata province. The decision held that Article 3 did not include Peruvian Tarata. The source totals cannot split the populations of occupied and unoccupied districts. [Treaty of Ancón, arts. 2–3](https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/Navegar?idNorma=220112), [Sinopsis, printed pp. 27 and 30](https://books.google.com/books?id=CiswAAAAYAAJ&pg=PA30), [1925 award](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1925v01/d204)

## Callao count

The 20 June 1905 census counted 33,879 people present in the historical constitutional province of Callao. The male count 16,992 comprises 16,201 communal and 791 special enumerations. The female count 16,887 comprises 16,622 communal and 265 special enumerations. The province includes islands and ships. The 1905 report cites an October 1889 law defining its territory. Its enumeration sections and population categories differ from the 1876 line of the Sinopsis, which reports 34,492 people in two districts. A complete boundary and population-definition crosswalk to 1876 is pending. The 1898 figure of 28,932 is listed under Callao city in the historical census compilation, distinct from the 1905 province. [Arca Parró derived citation, Table VI](https://www.colesp.org/images/pdfs/biblio/recursos/pease.pdf) Keep the 1905 control as a dated observation. The 1896 projection of 48,118 exceeds it by 14,239.
