# Argentina — principal occupation, 10 May 1895

The Second National Census, Volume II, Cuadros XXVI a–d, printed pp. 47–50 (Capital Federal) and 139–142 (Buenos Aires Province), tabulates each person aged 14 and over by a single principal profession. The census report describes how enumerators chose the occupation that provided a person's livelihood when they named several. The report lists women carrying out unpaid domestic work for their own household among residents without a recorded profession unless they named another occupation. The title and age rule can be checked against each local table and Volume II's Cuadro XI, p. CLXX, which independently matches the 455,032 and 569,737 figures. The suggested occupation distribution uses I less Hacendados (rural estate owners), II–IV, VI–XI, XII's Maestros de Escuela (schoolteachers) only, XIII–XIV and the amounts in admitted_xv and admitted_xvi. XVI is the census category of casual or irregular day labour (Jornaleros), with no sector inference. All-age observed_unknown includes ages below 14 outside the table, Hacendados, V (rentiers), XII's Estudiantes, XVII (a mixed residual), XVIII (no reported profession), and the unresolved capital counts below. See the column names for the original tabulated category labels (normalized spelling). No combination with the age, sex, religion, or literacy opening distributions is implied.

For Buenos Aires Province, IV = 11,880 and VIII = 3,636 are totals of the four printed sex-by-nationality columns. The provincial I–XVIII categories sum to the printed 14+ total. For the capital, I–XVIII printed subtotals sum to 454,918 against the published 455,032 grand total. The printed XV and XVI subtotals disagree with the sum of their four columns. Both original subtotals remain in the CSV, and the occupation allocation puts all of XV and XVI and the 114-person census subtotal shortfall in unknown. No capital population is reassigned to those groups.
