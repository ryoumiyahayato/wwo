# Argentina opening geography, 1900-01-01

`geography_crosswalk.csv` partitions the existing `argentina_1900` FT control of 4,624,686. The named census-scope areas receive 4,524,112. The Puna geography balance contains 749. A further 99,825 remain unlocated outside individual enumeration. The 23 rows represent 21 names or comparable combined scopes and two balances. `alsina_1901_geography.csv` transcribes the 25 source rows, which sum to the printed 4,926,913.

## Source and dates

- 1895-05-10 is 1,697 days before opening. Use the 24 individually enumerated rows from the INDEC retrospective of the 1947 census, vol. I, printed p. 1, Cuadro 1. They total 3,954,911. The comparability check uses the original 1895 census vol. I, pp. 5–9 and 16–17, territorial definitions and `Resumen general`. These distinguish the federal district from the adjoining province. [INDEC 1947 Cuadro 1](https://biblioteca.indec.gob.ar/bases/minde/1c1947t1master.pdf) · [Original 1895 geographic descriptions](https://biblioteca.indec.gob.ar/bases/minde/1c1895t1.pdf)
- 1901-12-31 is 729 days after opening. Use Juan A. Alsina, *Población, tierras y producción*, 1903, printed p. 20, `Superficie y población`. This is a contemporary published estimate. The volume compares its national estimate with the individually enumerated 1895 result on p. 18. Most printed area values repeat the 1895 area schedule, which identifies the statistical units. That repetition is no independent boundary survey. The primary text transcription was checked by summing all rows. A separate page-image check is pending. The publication gives no region-specific enumeration adjustments for the estimates. [1903 edition](https://play.google.com/store/books/details/Juan_A_Alsina_Poblaci%C3%B3n_tierras_y_producci%C3%B3n?id=X1ACAAAAYAAJ) · [Digitized text](https://es.scribd.com/document/371964277/Alsina-Juan-Poblacion-Tierras-y-Produccion-1903)
- 1914-06-01 is 5,264 days after opening. Its 7,885,237 individual census records and subsequent crosswalk help verify accounting. Its geography follows major changes in 1902. The 1901 estimate supplies the closer model endpoint. The 1914 national coverage figures remain part of the independently staged age model. [INDEC 1947 Cuadro 1 and its notes](https://biblioteca.indec.gob.ar/bases/minde/1c1947t1master.pdf)

## Geographic decisions

- The federal district incorporates Flores and Belgrano by 1888. Its separate 1904 population census on September 18 enumerates 950,891 residents. Linear local interpolation from 1895 to 1904 gives 806,365 for opening without national scaling. The nationwide share model gives 772,173. The difference gives a measure of timing and method sensitivity for the capital. `place:buenos_aires` fits the federal district. The Chandler urban estimate has a separate non-additive scope. [Buenos Aires boundary history](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/sites/default/files/media/document/2021/02/19/99d1893d1e5e727f0f60f129c5a0541185d9b2b3.pdf) · [1904 publication, p. XXIII, as located by Buenos Aires statistics office](https://revista.estadisticaciudad.gob.ar/ojs/index.php/poblacionba/article/download/172/222/728)
- The combined Córdoba, Santa Fe, Santiago del Estero and Chaco scope preserves the rows across internal boundary modifications. The published sources lack a published population crosswalk for the transferred strips. Law 3349 followed the 1895 census and authorized the Santa Fe–Chaco demarcation. The 1895 census also identifies an unresolved Córdoba–Santiago border that the legal record places in arbitration in 1896. Law 4141 modified Santiago–Chaco in 1902. The Córdoba and Rosario place markers refer to this statistical scope as locations. Retain their urban statistics independently. [Law 3349](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/norma-284519) · [Córdoba–Santiago legal history in Law 22789](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-22789-199434/texto) · [Law 4141](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-4141-285268/texto)
- The March 1899 award brought the Puna area into Argentine territory. Law 3906 created the Territory of Los Andes on 1900-01-09. Its later 1902 annexation of San Antonio de los Cobres came from Salta. The 1901 table predates that transfer. The 1947 crosswalk's 1914 Catamarca +378 and Jujuy +880 belong to Los Andes. Salta's 1914 crosswalk +1,229 comprises 268 Pastos Grandes and 961 San Antonio. A strict 1900 recast of 1914 would restore the 961 to historical Salta and keep the 1,526 from the 1899 Puna separate. Carrasco separately reports 1,149 in 1901 across the same three departments. A partial 1900 enumeration lists 507. The 1895 national census supplies no Argentine count for the 1899 acquisition. The blank early input in the CSV represents that lack of coverage. The 749 in the model represents the time-weighted contribution to the unexplained geography balance. [1899 award](https://legal.un.org/riaa/states/argentina.shtml) · [Law 3906](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-3906-285053) · [Law 4059](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/norma-285192) · [1914 departmental counts and published research](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0718-10432011000200010&script=sci_arttext) · [Carrasco 1901, reprinted in Table 3](https://www.mundoagrario.unlp.edu.ar/article/download/v06n11a02/1290?inline=1) · [Partial 1900 enumeration, Cuadro 1](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/300/30081510019.pdf)
- The Argentine-side national territory of Tierra del Fuego includes Isla de los Estados under the 1881 treaty. Use 477 in 1895 and 1,037 in the 1901 estimate. A 1971 official yearbook prints 447 in the early total but its three named department rows give 81 + 73 + 323 = 477. For 1914 the three domestic rows close at 532 + 414 + 1,558 = 2,504. The 16 Antarctic observatory residents are in the 1947 column. [1881 treaty, art. 3](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-1116-1881-216105/texto) · [Tierra del Fuego yearbook, Table 1](https://ipiec.tierradelfuego.gob.ar/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Anuario-Estadistico-1971-Tomo-I.pdf)
- Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut and Santa Cruz are Argentine-administered statistical areas using two pre-award sources. No international boundary adjustment has been applied to these values. The 1902 award identified disputed stretches from the Nahuel Huapi area to Patagonia. Keep the territorial leaves statistical and omit polygon bindings. The 1947 military-region recast is unnecessary with the two early endpoints. [1884 territories law](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-1532-48923) · [1902 award](https://legal.un.org/riaa/cases/vol_IX/29-49.pdf)
- Misiones is the Argentine statistical territory after the February 1895 Cleveland award. Mendoza and Pampa Central use the geography reported in 1895 and 1901. The 1907 law adopted the Mendoza–Pampa line that commissions marked in 1881. The model concerns historic statistical units and terrain alignments are unverified. ## Reproduce the opening allocation

1. Retain the national 4,624,686. Preserve the already modeled 99,825 coverage reserve. The 1895 additions to individual enumeration are 60,000 general omissions and 30,000 Indigenous people. The 1914 additions are 118,582 general omissions, 18,425 Indigenous people counted as groups and 20,000 remote residents, mainly in Formosa. Keep this reserve geographically unlocated and without age or sex assignment. [INDEC coverage and national controls, p. XXVIII](https://biblioteca.indec.gob.ar/bases/minde/1c1947t1master.pdf)
2. Divide each row's 1895 count by 3,954,911 and each 1901 estimate by 4,926,913. For the Puna balance, use a zero share of the 1895 national enumeration because it supplied no record for the area. Blend the two shares with later weight 1697/2426. Multiply by 4,524,861. Assign the integer remainder in descending order of fractional part and then ascending unit ID. The output sums to the national control. This is a share allocation estimate. A 1900 jurisdiction-level margin of error is unavailable.
3. `opening_coverage_reserve` flags the portion outside the age model. The 749 geography balance remains part of the opening age model. Capital Federal uses the city census shares from May 1895 and September 1904, in five-year age groups by sex. Buenos Aires province uses the 1895 and 1914 provincial census shares in ten-year age groups by sex. The model interpolates both sets of local shares to 1900 and retains unreported ages. It subtracts both local models from the national cells by age and sex, then applies the residual to the other census-represented accounts. Migration and differences among provinces have no quantified interval.

Step 3 describes v31. In v32 each of the 21 geographic accounts uses the eligible local census panels specified in `opening_age_profile_sources.csv`. The national censuses each contain 24 source regions. All 24 add to the corresponding national table by age and sex after regrouping the capital's five-year intervals. Some 1914 boundaries include population transferred after opening. The crosswalk uses the 1895 shares for nine affected accounts with 482,567 opening residents and uses two compatible dates for the other twelve accounts. Córdoba, Santa Fe, Santiago del Estero and Chaco are summed by age and sex before applying shares to their single comparable account.

The v32 national age and sex distribution sums the territory models and leaves 749 people in the unresolved Puna geography balance unclassified, alongside the separate 99,825 coverage reserve. The number with both age and sex is 4,502,031. The v31 national-share estimate was 4,505,257. The opening total remains 4,624,686. The difference in modeled age coverage reflects the use of independent local distributions and the unclassified Puna balance. No census observed the opening age structure directly. The geographic crosswalk retains the excluded 1914 panels as dated historical observations.

## Files

- `alsina_1901_geography.csv` contains the 25 reference rows from the contemporary estimate.
- `geography_crosswalk.csv` contains the dated geography allocation, component IDs and point associations.
- `capital_federal_age_sex.csv` contains the age-by-sex controls reproduced in the Buenos Aires municipal yearbook. Its 1914 results remain available as historical reference.
- `buenos_aires_province_age_sex.csv` records the separate provincial controls in the INDEC retrospective, Cuadro 3, printed p. 75.
- `provincial_age_sex.csv` records the other forty-four 1895 and 1914 source panels.
- `opening_age_profile_sources.csv` records the eligible census years and geographic exceptions for every opening account.
