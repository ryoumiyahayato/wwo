# Mysore census divisions

`india_mysore_census_1901_report` refers to T. Ananda Row, *Census of India, 1901, Volume XXIV, Mysore, Part I, Report* (Mysore Government Press, 1903). Ch. I §11 and Appendix A p. 34 identify the Eastern and Western Divisions and Bangalore Civil and Military Station as three disjoint parts of the census state. Appendix A to Ch. II pp. 73–74 quotes the 1891 census totals for each. Subsidiary Table D–1 includes both division totals in its full-population denominator. [1901 official report](https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10973/18854/GIPE-017294.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=3)

The census dates were 26 February 1891 and 1 March 1901. The official report compares the two counts using the same division names and identifies the station separately. Its Chapter I §17 describes the Appendix B comparisons on a common 1901 area. District and taluk boundary differences within the divisions limit further subdivision. [1901 official report](https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10973/18854/GIPE-017294.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=3) [1911 official report, Introduction §1](https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10973/18962/GIPE-016861.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=3)

The national retrospective table directly reports males and females for the Bangalore station at both dates. Division sex fields remain blank pending a direct table. [Census of India 1941, vol. I, part I, Table IV, p. 80](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/32422/download/35603/21325_1941_TAB.pdf)

The two divisions and the station sum to 4,943,604 and 5,539,399, matching the existing `raj_mysore_state` control in all-India Table II-A row 21. Keep that parent as a statistical aggregate of the five opening accounts, with its opening 5,470,284 and existing province-wide margins. Keep `place:bangalore` with the Mysore-administered city in the Eastern Division if that is its existing geographic meaning. The station was a separately counted British-administered assigned tract within the geographical Mysore census state. Its count is included exactly once. [1901 official report, Ch. I pp. 18–21, 34](https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10973/18854/GIPE-017294.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=3)

Part C, Table II, printed p. 24 disaggregates the Western Division. Hassan, Kadur and Shimoga close to 1,372,996 in 1891 and 1,463,407 in 1901. Those districts replace the Western Division in the opening ledger. The Eastern Division remains one account because its five district counts use a comparison boundary with 362 more people in 1891 than the parent control. [Talukwar Tables](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/32404/download/35585/138949_1901.pdf#page=40)

## United Provinces princely states

The 1901 all-India Table II returns Rampur and Tehri-Garhwal separately from the directly administered provinces. Their counts are 551,249 and 241,242 in 1891 and 533,212 and 268,885 in 1901. They close to the population used in both endpoint controls. Local male and female counts are preserved from the official 1941 provincial retrospective table. [1901 Table II](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/31349/download/34530/140099_1901.pdf#page=10) · [1941 United Provinces Table II, pp. 17–18](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/32515/download/35696/22421_1941_UNI.pdf)

Opening age bands use the applicable parent age tables. The locality rows in this collection keep the independently reported sex counts. Opening estimates use the two enumeration dates. Census error, famine and migration effects within the interval have no quantified bounds.
