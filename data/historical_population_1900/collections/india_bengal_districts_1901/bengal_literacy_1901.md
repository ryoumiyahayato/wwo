# Bengal 1901 literacy

E. A. Gait, Census of India, 1901, Vol. VI-A, The Lower Provinces of Bengal and their Feudatories, Part II, Imperial Tables, Bengal Secretariat Press, 1902. Table VIII, Education, Part B, Details for Districts and States, All Religions. Use the mutually exclusive Total Literate and Total Illiterate columns. The definition is ability to read and write a letter, all ages. Literacy in English and a vernacular can be counted twice in the language-specific columns. The population controls are Imperial Tables I and II, Calcutta city (with Fort and excluding the separately tabulated suburbs) and Dacca district (including Dacca city). [Official scan](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/30991.pdf). [Scan and OCR](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.55931). [1901 questionnaire](https://www.censusindia.mha.gov.in/census.website/en/CENSUS_ques).

Calcutta: the totals from Table VIII close to 847,796. The 32756 and 252444 female values follow the corresponding total and male columns, which recover the independent female population of 285200. The separate Calcutta Part IV report's OCR has a one-person mismatch in the illiterate total compared with Imperial Table VIII and a one-person difference in the quoted female literate count.

Dacca district: the printed literacy rows by age sum to 173345 (9862 + 26552 + 22798 + 114133), which agrees with 2649522 less the printed illiterate total of 2476177. Two cells with damaged OCR were reconstructed from the independent district totals and complementary sex columns. The 159389 literate men follow from 1312417 minus 1153028. The 1323149 illiterate women follow from 1337105 minus 13956. The two category totals close independently by sex.
