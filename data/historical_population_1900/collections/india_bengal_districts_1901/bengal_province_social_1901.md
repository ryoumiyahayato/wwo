# Bengal British Territory, 1901

The census date is 1901-03-01. The 48 census entries consist of 47 districts and Calcutta city. The 74,744,866 persons comprise 37,376,782 males and 37,368,084 females. The statistical abstract lists Bengal States separately, with 3,748,544 persons. [Provincial Census, Vol. VI-A, Imperial Tables I and II](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.55931). [Statistical Abstract, Thirty-ninth Number, 1905](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/statistics/1894_excel/index.html).

## Literacy

Imperial Table VIII, Part II(a), Vol. I-A, pp. 108–109, gives the `4. Bengal` control and 70,550,531 `Illiterate` persons. Its four all-person illiterate age subtotals are 21,258,899, 7,913,860, 6,049,736 and 35,328,036. Table 16 of the 1905 Statistical Abstract gives both sex totals and both literacy classes. In the DSAL transcription, rows 8 and 36, columns B through D, read population 37,376,782 and 37,368,084, `Illiterate` 33,388,189 and 37,162,342, and `Literate` 3,988,593 and 205,742. The census included all ages and counted people able to read and write a letter. [Imperial Table VIII scan](https://www.scribd.com/document/765937813/Census-India-1901-India-Tables). [1905 Table 16](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/statistics/1894_excel/1894.016.XLS).

The second CSV row subtracts Calcutta city and Dacca district from the British Territory control in every numeric column. It measures the combined 46 remaining census districts. The first row is the independent provincial control. Use either the province as a single distribution or the remainder together with the two separately observed districts. See `bengal_literacy_1901.csv` for the subtractions.

## Religion

1905 Statistical Abstract No. 39, Table 7, gives the province in the `Bengal` row under `PROVINCES.` The category sequence printed in the abstract is `Hindus.`, `Sikhs.`, `Jains.`, `Buddhists.`, `Parsis.`, `Muhammadans.`, `Christians.`, `Jews.`, `Animistic.`, `Minor Religions and Religions not returned`. Map the mixed last category to unknown. `Hindus.` is the published aggregate. Provincial Table VI, p. 26, prints a separate `Brahmo` cell of 3,118. Keep the abstract’s grouping when using this CSV. [1905 Table 7](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/statistics/1894_excel/1894.007.XLS). [Provincial Census, Vol. VI-A, Imperial Table VI, pp. 26–28](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.55931).

## Marriage

1905 Statistical Abstract No. 39, Table 11, `Distribution of Population according to Residence, Sex, and Civil Condition`, gives the all-ages `Unmarried.`, `Married.` and `Widowed.` entries in the `Bengal` row pair under `PROVINCES.` The DSAL transcription has the male counts in C11–E11 and female counts in C12–E12. The two sex totals are 37,376,782 and 37,368,084 in F11–F12. The independent 48-district and city controls match both counts. The abstract lists Bengal States on rows 34–35. [1905 Table 11](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/statistics/1894_excel/1894.011.XLS).

## Language

Table 21 lists the language ordinarily used. The 11 published Bengal categories in cells A36–B47 sum to 74,744,866. The table prints `Others` as a combined category. Bengal's published categories exhaust the independently confirmed territorial denominator. The game assigns the 1901 shares to the January 1900 provincial account. District language remains unassigned. [1905 Table 21](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/statistics/1894_excel/1894.021.XLS).
