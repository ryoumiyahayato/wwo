# Ordinarily used language in India, 1901

The CSV contains 98 language cells for 15 census territories. The census schedule asks for the language ordinarily used by each person. The 1905 Statistical Abstract publishes the principal languages in each territory. `Others` pools smaller reported categories. Its composition varies by territory. The abstract lists unreturned or unidentified languages outside the territory's listed category subtotal. Retain each printed label and both residual fields. [1905 Statistical Abstract No. 39, Table 21](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/statistics/1894_excel/1894.021.XLS). [Census of India, 1901 schedule](https://censusindia.gov.in/census.website/en/CENSUS_ques).

`returned_total` is the subtotal of the printed language cells. `observed_total` is the independently published all-person population in Imperial Table X, Part I, printed pp. 158–159. `observed_unknown` is the difference between those two values. The unknown counts are Coorg 9, Madras 799, United Provinces of Agra and Oudh 6,791, Bombay States 688, Hyderabad State 515 and Mysore State 36. The other included territories close directly to the all-person population. [Census of India 1901, Vol. I-A, Table X](https://www.scribd.com/document/765937813/Census-India-1901-India-Tables).

The opening ends of the same populations match those census controls. Agra and Oudh uses a peer-district sum of 47,691,782 and an independently retained 294-person comparison discrepancy. Its original unknown count remains 6,791 and `opening_endpoint_scope_unknown` records the extra 294. The opening allocates from the original census denominator and keeps the separate 294 residents unassigned. The other 14 territories have zero `opening_endpoint_scope_unknown`. Central India Agency includes Gwalior, Mysore State includes the Bangalore Civil and Military Station, and United Provinces States includes Rampur and Tehri-Garhwal. Apply each return once on the corresponding aggregate account.

Bombay Presidency's census language subtotal covers the British districts including Aden. A different source or an explicit Aden language subtraction is required to allocate the opening account excluding Aden. Bombay States is separately included in the CSV. The Madras States aggregate overlaps the independently allocated Travancore language return. The other Madras States need distinct local numerators or a verified subtraction under the same published grouping. Assam, Burma, the combined Punjab comparison area, and the Andaman and Nicobar comparison area have different enumeration scopes from the matching Table 21 headings.
